I was listening to Alex O’Connor’s podcast when a random thought popped into my mind. I had seen Alex talk about this extensively on his YouTube channel, and a whole lot of others as well. The thought had mainly to do with suffering. Would the world be better with or without suffering?

I thought about this, and a lot of it depends on one aspect of reality which I cannot answer: Is there life after death? Oh, how joyful it makes me to think there might be something after the fact. That even if life slips away somehow, someway faster than it does—would I get a second chance? Everything is science, everything is fact. I would like to employ the Dawkins technique here and argue nothing exists outside of reality. Nothing will ever exist outside of reality. There is no afterlife. There is no everlasting happiness.

Now that that is the case, I suppose suffering is not justified. Let’s try to break this down. I would like to start with nuance.

Take me, for example. I suffer a lot. I have accepted my suffering. I have seen what my suffering has caused. But will I say no to my suffering? I won’t. It is mine, and I own it. I have grown from it. I will grow from it. It has made me who I am. But yet, a part of me screams—would I still be writing this if I lost one of the dogs? Would I still be writing this if I lost Bella or if I lost Hobbes? That possibly is going to be the greatest blow to my system. I can’t believe this. But my brain says yes.

You’ll take the suffering if it makes you grow. If it means you’ll learn. You’ll take it. You’ll have to take it. You will have to experience life, Hemang. Anyway, sobs aside, let’s look at a few generic cases of suffering that I conjure up in my mind.

There is a person, X.

Person X is suffering.

Person X dies.

Unjustified. No afterlife, nothing matters. Life ceases with the breath.

Person X lives.

Person X is able to learn from it. Justified.

Person X lives.

Person X is made worse from it. Unjustified.

I don’t have a way to quantify the aggregates of all such cases, and all the fucking nuance humanity has with it. As a whole, though, I would prevent suffering in any way possible. I would assume the worst and denounce suffering as unjustified.

These wars—Palestine, Israel, Iran, Ukraine, Vietnam, Japan, Russia, America—they are all bad. So crude, the way I put it. But it is horrific. It is plain inhumane. I don’t even want to dissect this. War satiates my heart when it comes to this question.

Suffering is unjustified.
