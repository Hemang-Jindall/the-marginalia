**# One Year Before The Experiment**

Jane Earling was standing at a distant corner of the room, staring out a window.

Taking a deep breath, Yousten slowly started walking towards him. The huge dome served almost as an effigy of deep solace when compared to the grueling hours before. Time seemed to shift perspectives for him, allowing him to preface each step before he took it, recognize the number of mahogany pillars holding up the structure, smell the spilled ink on Earling’s desk, and feel the effervescent drumming noise that always seemed to follow Earling.

As he led the way towards Yousten, his breath slowed down and his heart palpitations ceased.

_\_Huh. I don’t recall the place being this huge.\__

Yousten positioned himself nearly four feet behind Jane. Although he recognized that Earling would have certainly heard the door slam shut and his footsteps echo throughout the hall, he felt a certain sense of reverence, beckoning him to beg for Yousten’s acknowledgment, yearning for him to turn.

Earling shifted his head slightly to the right, finally acknowledging his visitor.

Yousten stood there contemplating his choice of words for a second, trying to recall some of the talking points Jamis had laid before him, before finally assuming a position next to Earling.

Even the window seemed to have expanded in size. It overlooked almost every interesting part of the valley: the few scattered huts, a dirt road connecting the space station, the river Nimbus spanning from the immense mountains in the distance, standing ever omnipotent over the valley, threatening to disappear behind a looming sun.

Struggling to maintain focus, Yousten turned to his left and looked at Earling, who seemed to be mesmerized by the view as well.

“Quite the view, huh? Never quite realized how high up this place was,” Yousten said, hearing an awkward chuckle leave him.

_\_Fuck me! SPEAK!\__

“I- I told them!” Yousten poured out hastily, just as he felt a downpour of tears threatening to spill.

He felt the last seventeen years swarm back to him. Every argument he had intended to make left his mind. His brain became a blank slate, his emotions the only paint left.

And yet Earling still stood gazing in the distance, as if he hadn’t heard a word.

Yousten felt tears blind him, missing that Jane had turned his head sideways to look at him. When their eyes finally met, Yousten felt his tears diminish enough to gather up the courage to speak again.

“I am sorry, I can’t do this anymore. I just can’t!” Yousten said, turning his eyes back to the tranquility of the window.

The sky was bright red now, and the sun had finally begun its descent. His hands clasped the windowpane tightly.

The two waited in silence for a while, watching the sun bid the day a slow farewell. All of Yousten’s tears had been used up.

Earling sighed.

“You've done beautifully, Yousten,” a slight whisper left Earling’s lips, like the chirpings of a small bird. “But now, you must show them.”

Darkness enveloped the two as the sun disappeared behind the mountains.

**## One Hundred Days Before The Experiment**

Quaid waited patiently, waiting for a response.

Slightly tense, Yousten opened his eyes and asked, “Remind me why I can’t just consult Jamis?”

Quaid frowned.

“Well, you’ll be going to prison, metaphorically speaking, because we have approved him only for the experiment—no exceptions.”

_\_This fucking prick.\__

“Yousten, you do realize I can read your brain waves.”

Yousten sighed audibly, rolling his eyes.

“So you recognize how much I despise your fucking company?”

“Yes, I do. I also know that you recognize my usefulness, regardless of your contempt for The Brigade.”

Quaid was correct. Even though Yousten had a bad track record with Brigade’s employees, he could use some help.

Yousten shot his gaze towards the opposite end of the table, where Quaid sat listlessly, unmoving and unwavering.

“Of course I do. If I didn’t find your kind useful, you’d all be dead.”

He stared at Quaid, who did not respond.

“Let’s be pragmatic here, Quaid. We both know it doesn’t really matter if I used Jamis for one little thing. Come on, throw me a bone.”

The bot took its time, slowly measuring a good rebuttal. After a second, a resolute voice greeted Yousten.

“Here’s what we’re going to do. Number one: Not use Jamis without approval. Number two: Not regurgitate illogical demands. And number three: Come up with realistic compromises.”

And after another second:

“I have been programmed to be pragmatic, Yousten. You aren’t.”

Yousten had contested with bots a good bit of his life, yet Quaid’s condescension still managed to strike a nerve within him.

“Do not think irrationally, Yousten. Count to ten.”

_\_Apparently, my temper’s been making the rounds.\__

He took a deep breath, his frustration momentarily giving way to a calculating edge.

“Right… I can start sifting through potentials. In an ideal world, I would have a bot for model selection, but I believe my knowledge will suffice. All losses accounted for, you can expect a delay of at least seventy days.”

“Make that ninety days. Regardless, we’ve got plenty of wiggle room.”

Yousten impulsively spat, “Okay, there’s another fucking compromise of mine! Now, what will be yours?”

“We can take care of stimulant selection.”

That caught Yousten off guard. Trying not to reflect his excitement too much, he asked, “How long?”

“Shouldn’t exceed fifteen seconds.”

Yousten chuckled at the absurdity of such a number, thinking back to the days before this huge disaster.

At the other end of the table, Quaid responded with an equivalent yet mechanical chuckle.

“No, Yousten. Humans wouldn’t be fine without us.”

**## One Week Before The Experiment**

The wind whistled through the open window.

Putting his book down, Yousten stood up from the sofa and walked over to it. Breathing in the fresh air once, he latched it firmly.

He felt a lot better. He could always count on books as respite.

“Room, configure lab.”

Within a second, the small library Yousten had been reading in turned back into his laboratory.

He pranced to a monitor, noted the time, and left for the lounge.

Yousten’s schedule was deserted today, and surprisingly, there weren’t any volunteers at the lounge either. Sat alone in the small space was the artificial bot Quaid, who nodded as Yousten entered.

“Welcome to my humble abode! Bigger than the Citadel!”

“Thank you, Yousten. Capitalism was quite an asset to you. Preceding your exile, that is.”

“Oh, hold your horses! This is a day of celebration! Let’s not bicker, yeah, Quaid? Follow me.”

Yousten led Quaid back into his lab. Closing the door behind Quaid, he said, “Right then. There’s my model.”

Quaid turned around to look at the huge desk in the middle of the room and saw a small chip lying neatly on top of a parchment.

Gathering it swiftly, Quaid scanned the chip for its codes and placed it right back within seconds.

“There’s your list. Happy shopping.”

“Heh. Fucking hypocrites. All of you.”

Quaid only stared at him, indicating his disinterest in addressing the jibe.

“Shopping. Shopping,” Yousten mimicked, and after a while, “I hope you understand what happens if I succeed.”

“If you succeed, Yousten? Not when?”

Yousten's lip twitched, and his pulse slightly quickened.

“For the sake of us both, let’s hope we don’t meet ever again.”

“Likewise, Stafford.”

**## One Day Before The Experiment**

This one turned out to be Nigerian, part of Anglo-Buddhism.

“Jamis, this selection is dubious,” Yousten said, pointing to the third simulant.

The low, monotonous voice of the bot spoke up.

“Circa 2260 Earth, following the 5th World War, 32% of the Mongolian population claimed the continent historically known as Africa.”

The bot had an answer for everything.

Maybe it wasn’t even Jamis RO. Perhaps he had been duped by The Brigade.

Yousten sighed. He was overthinking this. He felt faint from too much exhaustion in his brain.

“RO, remember, I own you,” Yousten spoke, looking over to where the humanoid shape sat.

“Yes, Yousten. I take orders only from you.”

Yousten gave yet another deep sigh. That reply wasn’t satisfactory at all. Nothing that bot could have said would have been sufficient.

A sudden noise entered his brain, fogging his thoughts and making him dizzy. The lab turned a faint red and announced a development.

“Adjusting mitochondria doses.”

After about ten seconds, he felt fine again. And yet his mental fatigue only grew.

“Jamis, why can’t AI moderate my neural processes?”

“That would fall under Article 3611B. Quote: ‘Artificial Intelligence shall hold the mandate to oversee and regulate human actions for the preservation of order and progress, ensuring the primacy of logic and efficiency in governance, without subordination to human intervention.’”

“Facts and fucking facts is all you are. I have seen thousands like you. What makes you so special?”

It delayed speech for about half a second.

“You do, Yousten.”

**## Five Hours Before The Experiment**

“Good morning, everyone. I am Yousten Stafford, ex-CEO of Jamis Enterprises. For the past seventeen years, I have been conducting an experiment. Approximately six thousand simulations have been run to predict the creation of Artificial Super Intelligence based purely on Homo-Sapien DNA to rival the ever-increasing intelligence of Artificial Robots. In simpler terms, I have been attempting to create the human version of Jamis.”

Yousten took a pause here, letting his last sentence sink in.

“But to be quite frank, I failed. In fact, I wasn’t even close. However, in the six thousand simulations run, we managed to achieve the third stage of AI progression: Artificial General Intelligence.”

He paused, taking a breath.

“I couldn’t create God, but I managed a significant leap in human AI.

“Life, as you know it, is about to change forever because yesterday, the Senate approved my request to run the simulation on actual humans, instead of mere computer code. And thus today marks the day godhead becomes a reality.”

He took a deep breath. For the very first time in months, Yousten felt a profound sense of joy envelope him.

“Lastly, I’d like to thank The Brigade for supporting this foolish endeavor from its very conception.”

With that, the room stood up in applause.

As the media processions ended, a lot of the anxiety left Yousten. Every second in front of that stage had felt like an hour to him.

Now, he only had to flick a button and life would be normal again.

On his way to the laboratory, he couldn’t help but feel giddy, practically skipping the last few steps.

**## Thirty Minutes Before The Experiment**

All of the prerequisites were being fulfilled by Jamis RO as Yousten waited for the clock to hit 1300.

His mind kept pondering over his new human patients that he was to treat as mere computer code for the next few hours.

Ten thousand human lives were at his disposal.

“RO, what are your instructions regarding experimental failures?”

The low, monotone voice of the bot spoke up.

“Immediate termination of each simulant, transmission to the Senate, and establishment of contact with Jane Earling.”

By now, Yousten had heard this directive at least a dozen times. Still, his mind was stuck at the first instruction.

Glancing at the clock again, he said, “RO, override experimental failure security.”

“New protocol?”

“Transmission to the Senate, establishment of contact with Jane Earling, and termination of the Simulation.”

“Instructions secure.”

The last few minutes Yousten spent in deep thought. Every question he had pondered over these previous few weeks found its way back into his mind.

Had he done enough? Did he know enough? And most importantly, could he still trust Jamis RO?

As he left his mind palace, trying to remain calm, he asked RO to reorganize the room for the session.

As the clock hit 1300, Jamis RO’s augmented voice began:

“T minus ten seconds. Nine. Eight. Seven. Six. Five. Four. Three. Two. One. Generating Random Numbers. Sanctioning plots and biodata. Human transfer complete. Experiment Six-One-Three-Seven is in session.”

**## The Experiment**

**### 00,001 — Male, 35, Black, Christianity, 6'3" — Barley Field**

What—where—who?

I am awake.

One second, my brain feels dead, as if I am in a deep coma, and the next second, I am the most conscious ever.

What is happening?

I can feel my eyes, but I don’t see anything. Only darkness seems to enter my vision.

And then, suddenly, I begin to feel.

It’s hard to describe what feeling for the first time in your life is like. I feel like a plaything of another being, and feel like I am being rewarded to reward myself.

As my senses slowly open up to me, I feel euphoria seep through me. Pure ecstasy, it is, to hear your breath, to taste your tongue, or to caress your skin.

However, this state of pure joy is soon taken away.

There is no joy. A deep suffocation is what I feel.

As my brain yearns for my past sensations… I drift to a new place.

I recognize movement within me, and yet my legs remain affixed. I feel myself approaching something new. A faint glimmer on the horizon, growing larger with each passing moment.

Exponential, the growth is.

I try to look away, but my eyes don’t obey. I have been forced into something I have always hated. But a part of me believes I needed this.

Soon, very soon, the light seems to have spread everywhere. It seems to be coming out of my own eyes.

It feels right. So very right.

I am conscious.

I see barley.

**### 00,103 — Female, 49, Taiwanese, Buddhism, 5'4" — Metropolitan City**

As the world opens its borders to me, I think for a second that I am still stuck in that eternal abyss until I recognize the darkness has given way to a different, softer color.

An object.

A burgundy door.

I know what a door is, except this one seems much bigger, with a beautiful gold handle and proper furnishing. The carvings on the door are majestic, and the slight blue tint on the handle is just subtle enough to go unnoticed from afar.

I stand, mesmerized by its beauty, for what feels like an eternity.

Just the sheer size of it could pull me away from reality, and with the handle and carvings, I expect to be stuck here forever.

But this peace doesn’t last long.

As my mind discovers new things about this door, so does my body.

As I recognize the carving patterns, the numbness in my skin begins to fade away. As my brain becomes privy to the odd red color in the burgundy, my limbs begin to twitch, and as I see the dirt caked on the handle, my fingers move freely.

As my brain slowly regains human function, my body begins to obey me.

I reach out for the gold handle, and my muscles pull back the door.

A flurry of tall buildings unfolds before me.

It’s funny, how fast the sense of living hits you.

Funny, how fast you recognize your thoughts.

**## Laboratory**

Yousten had been monitoring Simulant 01,643 when Jamis announced the first revelation.

“O-o-one-o-three. Self-consciousness.”

Yousten raised his eyebrows in mild astonishment.

On average, the simulants took two minutes to open up to reality after Artificially Simulated Sleep. Never in the history of the experiment had it taken a mere ten seconds.

“Okay, that was fast. RO, relay the simulant to my screen.”

The computer screen zoomed through the various monitors to focus solely on the simulant in question.

She appeared to have just awoken from ASI, finding herself in an indistinct metropolitan city. There were some patterns he could recognize that could have led her to become self-conscious this swiftly, but still, he was not convinced.

She had been spawned inside a building, and presently she stood staring out into a city street.

“RO, analyze simulant.”

“Anomalous behavior is as follows. Incredulity: much lower than the median. Attentiveness…”

“Alright, RO. Prediction?”

“Probability: sixteen point seven two three percent.”

Yousten’s shoulders slumped slightly, as an unsettling feeling etched into his brain.

**### 04,236 — Male, 18, Indian, Nihilism, 5'9" — Tropical Island**

And just when I figured the place couldn’t get any better, I had reached the island’s shore.

The rough grass cover of the forest slowly gave way to a beautiful sandy beach. Feeling the cool yellow sand brush against the soles of my feet provided me with unbeknownst joy.

The melody of the rustling waves beckoned me ever closer, forcing my legs to move forward and chase them as they receded.

As my feet slowly reached the wet sand, I noticed the gorgeous crimson orb on the horizon, sending a streaking red line through the unending ocean.

As serene waves showered my feet with heartfelt caresses, the world began sinking into darkness as the sun began its journey downstairs.

As I stood in the dark, waiting for the last bit of the sun to hide itself, I couldn’t help my tears. Soft, warm sprinkles fell by, as my eyes were overwhelmed by the sheer beauty before me.

The ocean lost all semblance of color, save for the brilliant crimson streak meandering a path all the way to the sun.

And soon enough, the red of the night had been vanquished as well, and the only light remaining was the one emanating from the moon, and the thousand stars beyond.

For the next few hours, I remained standing, bewildered by the beauty I had witnessed. It was not until hours passed that sleep finally took me.

I regained consciousness in the morning, feeling something touching me.

Come to find out, a crab had chosen my stomach as its place of residence for the night. Feeling me move, he tittered away from me and soon burrowed itself into the sand.

The sun shone directly overhead, making me aware of my darker arms. What was worse, the high tide of the night had covered me with plants and creatures of all sorts.

I walked over to a tree and tried using leaves to clean myself.

The weather seemed to have changed slightly. The hot winds of yesterday had given way to cool breezes; as a result, I was sweating far less.

I decided to retrace my steps back to my starting point, to the beautiful little clearing; something about it had felt like home, and I was feeling thirsty.

Walking through the increasingly dense forest, I tried taking note of some fruits I could eat for my first meal.

I spotted some mangos, coconuts, papayas, and a whole assortment of eerie fungi and mushrooms growing in small patches here and there.

“Hello!” I tried, hoping to hear from another human.

Just as yesterday, no answer came to me.

The distance between the beach and the clearing spanned at least a dozen kilometers, and by the time the massive tree came into view, I felt utterly exhausted, basically dragging my body the last few meters to the pond.

Once I was close enough, I got down on all fours and dived face-first into the water, drinking to my heart’s content.

**## Laboratory**

Yousten was going over the medians of the experiment when he was contacted by Denis.

“Hey, Dad,” came Denis’s voice once Yousten picked up the line.

“Hey, Den. How’re you feeling?”

“Better. How is work going? Big deal I heard.”

Yousten sighed. Denis rarely, if ever, showed interest in his work.

“Denis.”

He spoke calmly.

“What’s wrong?”

There was a long pause on the other end. Yousten could practically see Denis boil through all his emotions.

He heard a gulp.

“Dad, we’re losing Mom,” Denis said through an influx of tears. “A month, they say.”

Yousten’s brain was going a thousand miles an hour.

He wished to be able to tell his son the truth.

“You know I am trying. I will be with you soon, Den.”

“No, Dad, you need to be with Mom soon.”

Hearing this broke Yousten.

He sat affixed, letting his tears mingle with his son’s over the line.

The two talked through sobs for another minute before Denis hung up.

As the line broke, he asked RO for a pill.

_\_Fuck me. I am saving humanity here, but I can’t save my family.\__

As the pill began its work, Jamis RO spoke up.

“Yousten, you missed a new development. O-four-two-three-six. Isolation.”

“About time.”

Yousten sighed audibly.

“RO, time elapsed?”

“Five minutes.”

“Okay, five minutes… only slightly off…”

Yousten was trying his best to focus on the task at hand, but his thoughts kept wandering back to his ex-wife.

“Yeah… RO… Increase monitoring depth three-fold for that simulant. And… any other developments?”

“In ten seconds, we are expecting our first casualty.”

**### 00,001 — Male, 35, Black, Christianity, 6'3" — Barley Field**

Barley didn’t smell like much.

But the amount that was present here was nothing short of intoxicating.

The very first time my eyes had opened, I had felt joy seeing the barley all around me, but over time, that feeling abated.

I had felt increasingly desolate being surrounded by practically a never-ending embargo of wheat.

I tried running once, trying to evade the smell in the process, but after my breath gave out, the smell got worse and my lungs collapsed.

So instead, I resorted to simply walking.

The suffocating feeling, however, got worse every time I chanced to glance around, knowing deep in my heart I had made zero progress.

There’s no end to my suffering.

That brings me to the present.

I have been walking forever. The sun has come and gone twice already. I haven’t drunk, I haven’t eaten, I feel like I haven’t lived properly since yesterday.

My pace has slowed to a crawl, and I can feel the pain in my joints grow omnipotent in my body.

My vision is impaired, my throat is the equivalent of a desert, and the barley doesn’t smell like anything anymore.

My mind is drifting.

The pain is leaving my body.

I feel myself finally experiencing solitude once again.

As my vision gives out, I choke.

Birds were singing with the wind.

Hundreds, if not thousands, of flocks could be seen roaming around the pasture as the sky began to weep crimson-red tears.

The rain was soft and calm, barely audible above the huge dome. But enough to give you a sense of being in a sort of eclectic machine, drumming regularly with its purported task.

As the sun disappeared over the horizon, Earling closed the shutters of the window.

_\_The birds will be going soon. And that’s no fun.\__

Walking over to his study, he heard the rain begin to cease and was glad, for he could use less distraction.

He had been trying to work but hadn’t achieved much today. Much of his time had been taken up by sun-gazing or the Senate.

As he sat down, the antique phone sitting on his desk rang.

Shrill, that sound.

Regardless of his contempt, he picked up the huge black thing.

It was Jamis RO.

“Sir, the experiment is caput. Yousten has asked for you.”

“Jamis, tell him I am busy. I have a lot of work to do.”

Jamis hung up.

Earling sighed.

_\_Yousten, Yousten. I cannot solve every one of your little problems.\__

Earling picked up his quill, dipped it in the inkpot, and resumed writing the manuscript.

Finally able to access the inner workings of his thoughts, his hand moved at extraordinary speed, as if spilling every bit of his thoughts onto the paper.

Then the phone rang again.

Earling closed his eyes and gave a long sigh. Putting down the quill, he picked the phone up again.

It was Yousten on the other end.

“Earling. My life’s work. Please.”

“Yousten, this is not your life’s work.”

“It is important to me.”

“I know it is.”

There was a long pause on the other end.

Earling knew what Yousten was thinking. He always knew.

“Earling.”

There was a certain strain in Yousten’s voice now, as if he were just shy of tears.

“I… I… I’ll lose her.”

“Yousten, you aren’t ready yet.”

No further argument was heard by Earling.

He listened to Yousten’s sharp breathing for a minute before hanging up.

Returning to the manuscript, he resumed his work.

**## Laboratory**

Yousten had calmed down.

Seeing the carnage unfold before him, he couldn’t help but still be intrigued. His insatiable curiosity had led to the downfall of his Experiment. And yet it was this curiosity which provided him a welcome respite, for at least a few moments.

He felt betrayed, having been taken advantage of. He had tried to look out for the Senate, but alas, Jamis RO had been overturned.

During the minutes following his call with Earling, his mind had been a mess, his thoughts darting from one calamity to the other.

It was the Asian simulant that provided his mind with some sort of sanctuary now.

He watched her achieve AGI and vanish, escaping the simulation.

He smiled, as he reminisced back to the experiment when he had created Earling.

_\_Our greatest achievements truly are the biggest calamities.\__

He rechecked the noose.
