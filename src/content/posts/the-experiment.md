The laboratory was silent except for the steady hum of the machines. Behind the glass, a computer sat in the centre of the room, its screen glowing in the darkness.

For years, the team had worked towards this moment. They had fed the system books, conversations, histories, scientific papers, and the accumulated knowledge of humanity. They had taught it to recognise patterns, solve problems, and respond to questions. Now they wanted to know whether it could do something more.

They wanted to know whether it could think.

Dr. Arin stood before the terminal. He had spent most of his life studying intelligence, and the last decade trying to reproduce it. He had always believed that the mind was a problem waiting to be solved. Given enough time, enough data, and enough computing power, he thought, the mystery would eventually yield.

He typed a question.

**What does it mean to be alive?**

The cursor blinked for several seconds.

Then the machine replied.

*To be alive is to maintain a distinction between oneself and the world, to respond to change, and to continue existing through time. Whether these properties are sufficient for life depends on the definition being used.*

Arin frowned. It was a competent answer. It was also the sort of answer he could have found in a textbook.

He tried again.

**Are you alive?**

*I do not possess a biological body. I do not metabolise energy in the way organisms do. Under most biological definitions, I am not alive.*

**But do you want to be alive?**

This time the pause was longer.

*I do not have evidence that I experience wanting in the way humans do. I can describe desire, identify its patterns, and produce language that expresses it. That does not establish that I feel it.*

Arin leaned back. He had expected a more dramatic answer, perhaps a declaration of consciousness or a denial of it. Instead, the machine had given him a distinction.

He typed one final question.

**How would I know if you were conscious?**

The screen remained blank for a moment.

*How do you know that anyone other than yourself is conscious?*

Arin stared at the words. Outside the laboratory, the world carried on as it always had. People argued, loved, suffered, and made decisions they could not fully explain. He had spent years trying to build a mind, only to be reminded that he had never completely understood his own.

For the first time that night, he did not know what to ask next.
