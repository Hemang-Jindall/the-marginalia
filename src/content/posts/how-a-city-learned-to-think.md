Anaximenes was the pupil of the second pre-Socratic Greek philosopher, Anaximander. And just like the two before him, belonged to Miletus. I wonder what it about this port city was. Maybe it was happen-chance, Thales shows up somehow and thus tutors the next generation and so on. But still, it’s so interesting to note how this one small village (compared to contemporary cities) produced some of the earliest philosophy.

Returning to Anaximenes and his philosophy, unlike his predecessor who dipped his toes into biology, physics, metaphysics, cosmology, and geology, Anaximenes was rather curt and focused in a few parts of philosophy. Born in 586 BC, Anaximenes had been taught well and good about the way to think. His death marks nearly a century since the birth of the first philosopher.

Where Thales held the elusive “principle” to be water, and Anaximander purporting it to be all the elements, Anaximenes chooses air. In his views everything that has existed and will exist is either air or has been derived from air. He assigns a very unique dynamic property to air; it moves constantly, changes constantly, is hotter here and now, colder there and then, compressed and rarefied; all these changes produce the various other elements like water, fire and earth.

Another interesting aspect of air was his explanation of the heavenly bodies: Air is risen from the earth and is rarefied to produce fire and earthy objects appearing as Sun and Moon. Into this, he goes into much detail, explaining his philosophy decisively.

Much like Anaximander, Anaximenes, too, tried to explain atmospheric phenomena. Winds are generated when condensed air is driven along which when further condensed produced clouds; from the clouds even more compressed air gives us water, then subsequently hail and snow.

That was the philosophy of Anaximenes. I believe his ideas were slipshod compared to his predecessors’. Regardless, one of the most interesting aspects of philosophy (at least to me) is how different thoughts the predecessors have compared to their successors. They learn from their gurus, and then completely spit in their philosophies. That is truly wonderful.
