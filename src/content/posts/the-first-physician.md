Pythagoras, the most celebrated pre-Socratic, had a pupil by the name of Alcmaeon, who became the first of a distinguished line of Greek philosopher-physicians.

In fact, he is known to have written the very first book on natural science. He held the Sun, the Moon, and other heavenly bodies to possess an eternal nature, always in constant motion. But he didn’t just look up at the sky; he dug deep into our insides as well. He considered souls to be immortal, similarly ever-moving, just like the celestial bodies, whereas he held the body to perish.

But above all, he was a medical man. When the heart ruled as the conveyor of all rationale in a human being, Alcmaeon put forth the brain as the administrator. He also held that all the senses were connected to the brain. The brain monitored hearing, which he believed occurred through echoes inside hollow parts of the ear; smell, which he believed occurred when we suck in air through our nostrils to the brain; and the other senses (except the sense of touch).

He also stated that man perceives and understands, whereas animals merely perceive. He wrote about the health of man too, along the lines of equality of opposites maintaining good health, such as hot and cold. Lastly, a quote has been attributed to him:

> It is easier to be on your guard against an enemy than against a friend.
